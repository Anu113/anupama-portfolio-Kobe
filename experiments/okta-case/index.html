Okta's admin console, rebuilt in place, with 32% less admin time

Okta's admin console was built for companies with hundreds of people and was being sold to companies with tens of thousands. Four squads rebuilt it on the live product, so no customer had to migrate. I set the design direction and made the case for funding.

01The problem

The console was built for one person at a time. Enterprise admins work on whole groups.

It was a problem of scale, not of screens. At forty thousand people the question is "everyone in Finance who still has Salesforce", and every flow assumed one person. New enterprise customers took 14 weeks to set up, [X] confirm and four squads patching their own areas pulled the console further apart with each release.

02Goals

Four things every squad's work had to hold to

  1. Groups, not people

    Change access for everyone who shares something, in one step.

  2. Nothing moves on a bad day

    No migration date. Admins open this during incidents.

  3. One console, not four

    Four squads, one set of words and patterns.

  4. Accessible from the first build

    Keyboard and screen reader before anything shipped.

04The plan

We rebuilt it in place instead of launching a new console.

Engineering preferred a new console behind a flag and a switch-over day: easier to build, easier to demo. I argued against it. Admins open an identity console when someone has left or something has leaked, and that's the worst day for every button to move.

Migration dayEvery button moves on one date. The old console lives on for months, getting its own fixes.

07The worst day

Designed for the day something leaked

Every choice was tested against one day: the one an admin opens the console in a hurry.

  • Nothing moved on a date we picked

    Areas changed one at a time, next to the old ones.

  • See the count before you commit

    Every bulk change shows who it reaches first.

  • Keyboard first

    Bulk actions tried by screen-reader users before they shipped.

  • Every state in words

    Each state had a text equivalent, not colour alone.

08Outcome

Admins got faster, and no one had to migrate.

32%Less time on admins' four busiest tasks
0Customers asked to migrate

What changed beyond the console:

  • Every squad built on one access model. [X] confirm
  • One design system served the old console and the new one for over a year.
  • Crits ended with a decision and an owner, not a list of opinions.
  • Every designer watched one admin research session a month, whatever their area.

09What I'd do differently

Two people on the system, from day one

One designer carried both versions for two quarters. My plan put them there; I should have asked for a second at the start.

Ask leadership sooner

I waited for research before asking for funding. It cost a quarter. Now I'd ask early and let research shape the plan.
