Privacy settings people can read

PayPal's privacy settings were a list of legal controls most people never got past. I led the redesign into one page people can read, launched to more than 80 million people.

Context

The settings grew one control at a time.

A list of controls in legal language, with no structure underneath. New features and new GDPR and CCPA requirements had nowhere to go, and customer service heard the same privacy questions again and again.

75%of settings traffic stopped on the first page

Make managing your data easy, build trust, and meet the law, all on the same screens.

Research

Three problems, and each one became a part of the redesign

Four closed card sorts, a year of settings traffic, and sessions with customer service and the product teams that needed a setting here.

Where privacy questions come up

Finding 1 → One page, three headings

No structure, no room to grow

Controls added one at a time, in jargon. New settings had nowhere to go.

Finding 2 → Settings that explain

No transparency

Finding 3 → Data rights you can use

Nothing explained

Nothing told people what their data rights were, or how to use them.

Key interface decisions

Record · 6s

Scroll the revised page, tap one setting, back. Shows "one tap away".

01

Headings replaced a layer of sheets

In rapid research the groups worked and the sheets didn't: they added a tap before any real setting.

Record · 5s

Tap "Update your data", then the Emails link at the bottom.

02

"Update your data" replaced "Correct your data"

People read "Correct" as something other than updating their details. Marketing email got its own link, where people looked for it.

03

A white sub-navigation replaced blue on blue

Many people never saw the old one. Up to 85% better on some tasks in unmoderated tests.

Feature 2

Settings that explain

Each setting says what it does, what it's set to, and what changes if you switch it.

Problem: people couldn't tell how their data was used, or which controls they had.

Becomes a loop · the 8s Shopping recording

Key interface decisions

01

A toggle that says what's on replaced one "Turn off" button

It explains how it works with an example, and ends on "We never sell your personal information."

02

Explanations in place replaced a trip to settings

Most privacy questions start mid-task. Offers carry a link to their setting, and a card explains why you see them.

Feature 3

Data rights you can use

Get a copy, clean up, or delete, each saying what you'll get and what you'll lose.

Problem: nothing told people what their data rights were, or how to use them.

Becomes a loop · the 8s clean-up recording

Key interface decisions

01

"Clean up your data" came before "Delete and close"

The old flow had one path. Now face scan, marketing, package tracking and third-party data can go one at a time.

02

Grouped data types replaced a long checklist

Each says what it's for and what's included, and connected apps (Xoom, Venmo, Honey) are listed.

Record · 5s

Reach the end of delete, hold on the line about what PayPal must keep.

03

The delete screen says what the law keeps

Deleting can't remove what regulation requires PayPal to retain, so the screen says so before you start.
