02

Two sides, stated once, paid off in every section

The case has a two-sided problem: people who want to understand their settings, and a regulation that decides what must be said and kept. Today only the first side carries the story; the regulation side fades after the hard call, and "Your data rights" reads as an add-on.

Schneider: a booth visitor (3 seconds) against an operator (a whole shift), set up once and returned to in every section.

01 The situation

One screen, read by two very different readers

The person changing a setting decides the hierarchy. The regulation decides what the screen must say: if it hides what PayPal keeps, it doesn't matter how easy it reads.

A PayPal customer
One tap

What is PayPal doing with my data, and how do I change it?

  • Find any setting from the privacy page
  • See what it's set to, and what it does
  • Get a copy, or delete part, and keep the account
GDPR and CCPA
Every screen

Is everything disclosed, and is what must be kept, kept?

  • Say what's collected, why, and whether it's shared
  • Honour access and deletion requests
  • Name what PayPal must retain, not leave it silent

04

One teamwork moment instead of a list

"My scope" lists 3 PMs, 2 privacy officers and 15+ engineers. That's the size of the room. A Staff role is about what happened in it: one disagreement, the call you made, what it cost. Only you know the real one, so this is a template.

Schneider: "Four decks became eight. I sent the dashboard at 80%, said plainly that the scope had doubled, and we agreed what to drop."
