Experiment · PayPal Privacy screens · review · 2026-11-18

Are the Privacy screens good? Mostly, yes. Here's where they aren't.

An honest review of the shipped screens in the case, against the references (Meta Privacy Center, Facebook's privacy team, the CHI 2023 ad-settings study, Policy to Pixels), then an iteration of each weak spot, drawn beside the shipped screen.

The story: Shipped is what launched at PayPal; Iteration is the next round, on the screens that change. Screens that hold up stay Shipped only. The outside case studies are early references, not the story. Blue is the iteration; [X] is yours to supply; yellow "example" tags mark placeholder values.

The verdict

The structure is senior. The screens stop one step short.

The decisions that matter most are right, and some are ahead of what Meta and Google published. The gaps are all the same kind: a screen explains, then leaves the visitor without the control or the state they came for.

Strong, keep as is

  • One page, three groups, one tap to any setting. The CHI study found links straight to a setting raised findability by up to 41%; you shipped that.
  • Two ways to delete, "Clean up" first. No reference has this. It's your best decision.
  • A switch that says its state in words. "Personalized deals and offers is turned off." Withdrawing is as easy as agreeing.
  • "Personalize your experience" inside the offers. The CHI study's best-liked entry point was exactly this: inside the ad itself.
  • Data types grouped with "What's included". Better than Google's Takeout list.

Weak, fixable

  • The hub doesn't show what anything is set to. Your craft heading promises "what it's set to"; the hub shows only what each setting does.
  • What data is used is behind "Learn how it works". The best copy in the flow is one tap away from the switch it explains.
  • Explanations end on "Got it". "How it works" and "Shopping and Privacy" explain, then offer no control.
  • The yearly reminder asks a question it can't answer. "Still happy getting shopping deals?" then "Review your settings".
  • Big illustrations push the first setting down. On the hub, the first row starts around 55% down the screen.

A real problem

  • The case says the delete screen names what PayPal keeps. The redesigned "Clean up your data" screen doesn't. That line is only on the old "Delete and close" screen. An interviewer who taps through will find it.
  • "Clean up" copy reads as spin. "Improve your experience… over-retained, outdated and irrelevant data." Deletion framed as a benefit, with legal jargon.
  • Unfinished copy in a shipped frame. "Third-party acquired profile data [Content FPO]".
  • The data request ends with no expectation. No time to ready, no status, though your case lists six steps and "76% faster data access".
