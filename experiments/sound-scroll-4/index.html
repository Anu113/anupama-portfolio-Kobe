Sound, round 11 · feel good

Sounds that feel good

This round starts from research on what makes a sound pleasant, rather than from what would be fun to build. What the studies agree on:

  • Round, not sharp. The more high treble a sound has, the less pleasant people rate it. That's why the ticks, crackle and shaker of rounds 9–10 felt off: they're nearly all treble. (Sorama, indoor soundscapes study)
  • Smooth, not rough. Fast wobble or beating reads as harsh or urgent. The old hum was built from slightly out-of-tune buzzy tones, which beat against each other; that's the "intense". (psychoacoustic sonification)
  • Consonant, in the middle register. Major chords, fifths and octaves, kept off the lowest notes (where they get muddy and rough) and the highest (where they get sharp).
  • Wood over metal. In one study a major melody on a marimba was rated the happiest of the instruments tested, and bells were rated less pleasant than chimes because they're brighter. (timbre and emotion, non-sustaining instruments)
  • Water calms. Natural sound helps people recover from stress, and across studies water shows the biggest effect on mood. (Alvarsson et al. 2010)
  • Comfort, and attention only when needed. Google's Material guidance says UI sound should feel comforting and secure, and only ask for attention when it's needed. Microsoft's says that when in doubt, leave sound out. (Material, Microsoft)
A · Brook
A small stream, with the treble cut so it's round rather than hissy. It flows while you scroll and goes quiet when you stop. This is the water the research favours, as a stream rather than the drop or rain you passed on.
B · Soft chord
The hum done the way the research says: a major chord (D, A, D, F♯) in pure tune, in the middle register, round tones with no buzz and no beating. It swells in as you scroll and fades when you stop.
C · Marimba
Soft-mallet marimba notes on a major scale, wandering up as you scroll down and down as you scroll up. At most a couple of notes a second, never the same run twice. Wood, not bell.
D · Settle
Silent while you scroll. When you come to rest, a soft major chord on a felt piano, as if the wall has found its place. It only speaks when it has something to say.
E · Chord that settles
B and D together: the soft chord while you move, and the piano chord as you land.
