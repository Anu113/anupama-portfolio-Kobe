Case tags, without the lime

The lime appears in one place: the tags beside the company name at the top of every case study. Six replacements, each on the real hero, in light and dark.

01

Project hue

Recommended

The tag takes the company's own hue, the same sky / lilac / butter that sits behind its cover. Colour stays with the work (rule 2), so no new colour enters the system, and the top of the page visually ties to the cover below it. Ink on every hue is 10:1 or better.

02

Hairline

Ink text in a 1px outline. The most neutral option: it reads as metadata, matches the site's hairline dividers, and re-inks in dark mode on its own.

03

Quiet chip

Grey text on the raised surface (--paper-2, the image-mount grey). Softest of the six: it steps back so the title leads.

04

Just type

No chip at all: grey mono labels in faint brackets, as in carlthomasiv.com's row, the reference the case type already follows. Most typographic, least UI.

05

Clay dot

Grey labels, each led by a small terracotta dot. The accent stays a mark, never text, so it's within the "no clay on text" rule, and it echoes Moss's dot.

06

Ink chip

Paper text on solid ink, inverting in dark mode. Keeps the lime's weight without the colour, though three dark blocks may outshout the company name.
